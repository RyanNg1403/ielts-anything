# Source register and research evidence

Checked 11 October 2026. Links point to primary publications or official partner pages. Search excerpts were useful for discovery but were not treated as equivalent to a verified full document. No SEO “band 9” answer was admitted as a scored anchor.

## Trust and confidence

Trust order: official IELTS partners → authentic Cambridge books → IELTS Research Reports/peer-reviewed research → identifiable practitioner accounts. An official lesson plan is useful teaching guidance, but its templates and historic tips are not necessarily current internal marking rules. `official`, `examiner-claim`, and `unverified` describe rule provenance; research findings additionally state venue, scope and access limits.

No identifiable ex-examiner claim was needed to establish the accepted rules. Unresolved details were left unresolved rather than filled with blog advice. Proposed counting, generator density and model validation procedures are project recommendations, not IELTS policies.

## Research literature: findings and practical limits

### R1 — Descriptor revision validation

[Clark, Tasviri & Bruce (2023), IELTS writing scales review and update: summary overview](https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/ielts-writing-scales-review-and-update-summary-overview). Official IELTS research publication. Landing-page summary inspected: the project ran from 2019 to May 2023. Phase 3 used 103 examiners and 882 responses; Phase 4 compared ratings of 1,000 responses by 100 examiners under old and adjusted scales. This supports the revised edition's operational provenance and structured validation, not a claim that any untrained model using the PDF reproduces examiner decisions. No detailed coefficients are asserted from the landing summary.

### R2 — Human coherence/cohesion rating

[Cotton & Wilson (2011), An investigation of examiner rating of coherence and cohesion in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2](https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/an-investigation-of-examiner-rating-of-coherence-and-cohesion-in-the-ielts-academic-writing-task-2). Official IELTS Research Report. Summary inspected: think-aloud/interview work with 12 examiners and 10 scripts, plus 55 examiners rating 12 scripts. Reliability was acceptable across criteria; CC was slightly less reliable than LR/GRA and not significantly different from TR. Examiners found CC harder. This is historical human-rating evidence, not a contemporary model benchmark. [Full report](https://ielts.org/cdn/Research/investigation-of-examiner-rating-of-coherence-and-cohesion-in-ielts-academic-writing-task-2-cotton-et-al-2011.pdf).

### R3 — Memorisation and authentic proficiency

[The effect of memorized learning on the writing scores of Chinese IELTS test takers (2009)](https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/the-effect-of-memorized-learning-on-the-writing-scores-of-chinese-ielts-test-takers). Official research summary inspected. Profiles of 233 retired scripts separated potentially generic/native-like chunks from other language. Generic material and copied-question use did not simply decrease at every higher band. Use this to motivate cautious authenticity diagnostics; it does not establish a universal template penalty or a reliable automatic plagiarism detector.

### R4 — Presentation and construct-irrelevant variance

[Annie Brown (2003), Legibility and the rating of second language writing](https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/legibility-and-the-rating-of-second-language-writing-an-investigation-of-the-rating-of-handwritten-and-word-processed-ielts-task-2-essays). Official report page inspected. Addresses handwritten versus word-processed T2 assessment and the need to examine presentation effects. No numerical result is inferred from the introductory summary. Relevant to transcription quality, and not justification for assigning handwriting penalties to typed text.

### R5 — GPT scoring bias against official book labels

[Chen, Zhou & Prado (2025), ChatGPT-3.5 as an automatic scoring system and feedback provider in IELTS exams](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/ijate/article/1496193). Peer-reviewed International Journal of Assessment Tools in Education, 12(1); publisher abstract inspected. Based on pre-rated preparatory-book essays, the model tended to score below official raters; revision feedback was also studied. This contradicts a universal assumption of leniency. Model, prompt, corpus and historic rubric context limit transfer; no uninspected numeric agreement statistic is reported here.

### R6 — Dimension-specific prompt and exemplar effects

[Wang & Zhang (2026), A study on the reliability of ChatGPT-based English writing assessment: Internal and external perspectives](https://www.castledown.com/journals/lea/article/view/lea.2026.103386), Language Education & Assessment 9, DOI 10.29140/lea.2026.103386, published 19 August 2026. Publisher abstract inspected: 120 IELTS-style essays, four analytic criteria, three repeated rounds, nine human scorers. Detailed prompts improved TR/CC consistency more than LR/GRA; exemplar training reduced human discrepancies but slightly weakened internal consistency in most dimensions. These are IELTS-style essays and human-panel scores, not automatically operational IELTS ground truth. No invented ICC values.

### R7 — Specificity and relevance of GPT-4 feedback

[The capacity of ChatGPT-4 for L2 writing assessment: A closer look at accuracy, specificity, and relevance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/annual-review-of-applied-linguistics/article/capacity-of-chatgpt4-for-l2-writing-assessment-a-closer-look-at-accuracy-specificity-and-relevance/DD86884CA838040DB2A910AE77995B05), Annual Review of Applied Linguistics, Cambridge publisher page. Methods/results excerpts inspected. The study deliberately supplied highest-band criteria rather than the full scale, focusing on qualitative assessment feedback. It is inappropriate to cite it as a complete IELTS band-score accuracy trial. Separate feedback fidelity from scoring validity.

### R8 — Criterion-based LLM grading lead

[Evaluating large language models for criterion-based grading from agreement to consistency (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00291-1), npj Science of Learning 9:79. Publisher search extract identifies IELTS T2 essays with official examiner labels and repeated-run evaluation. Full publisher page repeatedly failed to open. Retained as a primary-source lead, not used for numeric results or strong operational claims. Search discovery is not full-text verification.

### R9 — Mid-band compression: scope matters

[IELTS Writing Revision Platform with Automated Essay Scoring and Adaptive Feedback (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24460). Author preprint, not admitted as peer-reviewed evidence. Its abstract attributes mid-band compression to early rule-based cycles before a transformer regression approach. It does not demonstrate that all current LLM graders compress IELTS scores at 6–7. Retained specifically to prevent overstating that popular claim.

### Other research leads not promoted into findings

[IELTS Writing Tasks Evaluation Using LLMs (IEEE)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10908761) and [Evaluating GPT ratings of EFL writing: a scoping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1075293526000322) were found through primary-publisher searches. Full results were not verified; no numerical/model ranking claim is based on them. [Cambridge Examining Writing, scoring validity chapter](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/734968-silt-volume-26-examining-writing.pdf) is a relevant historical framework, not a current marking manual or a verified new calibration dataset here.

## Search coverage and remaining gaps

Search rounds targeted descriptors/revision; criterion means and rounding; length/counting/copying; bullets/headings/position/overview; official IELTS and BC scripts; Cambridge publisher sample resources; Task 1 visual families and Task 2 wording; IELTS-funded rater studies; and primary-publisher LLM scoring studies. Direct PDF retrieval corroborated the two admitted script collections and the rubric. Broken access, historic versions and secondary reposts were recorded rather than hidden.

Remaining gaps: a complete authorised verbatim descriptor reproduction; internal Writing rounding and exhaustive counting specifications; licensed Cambridge book-by-book marks; representative density statistics for all visual types; current analytic ground-truth labels; and a validated modern-model error profile. The five research documents are written, but these evidence gaps prevent an honest claim of a fully examiner-equivalent grader or validated indistinguishable prompt generator.
