# Cambridge Academic patterns: books 12–21

Research checked 11 October 2026. Books 12–20 are profiled from the user's local Academic PDFs and OCR sidecars; the complete replacement PDFs are primary for Cambridge 13, 15 and 20. Cambridge 21 is Ppo-only. Books 1–11 do not contribute to any count below. The per-test ledger in [corpus.md](corpus.md) supplies the book, test, question blocks and printed-page (or scan-page/Ppo-page) locator for each observation.

Counts describe this retired-paper sample. They are not quotas or evidence of current live-test frequencies. A *block* is a consecutive set of questions using one task format; a test can contain multiple blocks of the same type. For passage position, each block follows the printed boundaries recorded test by test in [passage_boundaries.csv](passage_boundaries.csv); no fixed split is assumed. Multi-answer MCQ means choose-two-or-more selections; counts are blocks, not selected answer slots.

## Listening

Across 40 tests, each paper has four parts of ten scored items. Part 1 is a transaction or everyday enquiry in a two-person exchange; Part 4 is a sustained information/lecture-style talk. The corpus records form, note, table or similar completion in P1 and P4 throughout the 40 sets. Parts 2 and 3 vary more: public information, route/map tasks, MCQ, matching and completion recur in different combinations. The paper-by-paper sequence and contexts are in the Listening bullets for each Cambridge test in [corpus.md](corpus.md).

The following task-family figures are **presence counts across all 40 tests**, coded from local Cambridge 12–20 PDFs/OCR and the four Cambridge 21 Ppo pages. A paper counts once per family even when the family occurs in more than one block; a paper may contain several families. Multi-answer MCQ means an explicit choose-two-or-more-letters block. Matching means assigning prompts to a supplied option bank; gap-based flow charts are coded as completion, and map/plan labelling is counted separately. Completion combines forms, notes, tables and flow charts. Local PDFs/OCR control when the older Ppo extract omits a block or conflicts with a complete replacement copy, including C13 Test 3 Part 2 and C15 Tests 1 and 3 Part 3. ## Writing

### Task 1

Using one dominant family per prompt, the 40 visuals divide into **22 numerical charts/tables**, **10 maps/plans**, and **8 processes**. Combined chart/table prompts are classified as numerical; a map remains a map even when it depicts several time states. The counts follow the Task 1 description in each test row in [corpus.md](corpus.md), with the page spans in the coverage table above. Cambridge 21's four visual labels come from Ppo captions because its images are absent; those four classifications are less secure than the 36 PDF-backed ones.

The data shapes vary within those families: one or more series/categories at different dates, paired charts or tables, a process sequence, and before/after or multi-stage site plans. There is no fixed number of series, categories or time points supported by this sample. An image should be checked in the PDF before a prompt is finalized; the ledger does not invent visual detail when an image is unavailable.

## Speaking

There are 36 PDF-backed Speaking samples, four per book for Cambridge 12–20. Each follows the familiar progression from personal/familiar Part 1 topics to one Part 2 cue card and then broader, related Part 3 discussion themes. The topics span daily life, people, places, work/study, technology, culture, cities, the environment and public issues; the ledger has the concrete topic set for every test. It is not a topic-frequency quota. C17 Test 4's formerly incomplete entry is now recovered from the local PDF/OCR (pp. 94–95). Cambridge 21 has no Speaking sample.

Across the 36 samples the prompt logic broadens more reliably than any particular topic: Part 1 invites a personal account; Part 2 sustains one example with supporting points and a final explanation; Part 3 generalizes to social patterns or policy. Speaker answers are not supplied, so no candidate response register or answer-length model is inferred from the prompt pages.

## Interpretation boundary

Cambridge describes its books as authentic practice papers; IELTS publicly describes pretesting and balancing of difficulty, task types, topics, genres and Listening voices. This paper corpus helps observe released formats, but does not disclose operational item statistics, confidential writer rules or current live-paper frequencies. See [sources.md](../sources.md) and [gaps.md](gaps.md). The official Cambridge IELTS 20 product page states that the book includes four complete examination papers and supporting audio/video/key materials; this confirms book scope, not any specific frequency reported above.
