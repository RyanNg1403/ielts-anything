# IELTS Speaking: construction rules

Checked 11 October 2026. Shared by Academic and General Training. [Evidence register](sources.md). Examiner research describes dated practices; it does not expose the complete current confidential examiner handbook.

## 1. Official format and assessment

| Rule | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded examiner–candidate interview, three parts, 11–14 minutes overall. | confirmed-official | [F-S] |
| Part 1: introduction/identity check and interview on familiar everyday topics; 4–5 minutes. | confirmed-official | [F-S] |
| Part 2: individual long turn; one minute to prepare with notes, one to two minutes speaking, possibly one/two rounding-off questions; 3–4 minutes including preparation. | confirmed-official | [F-S], [P-S] |
| Part 3: related discussion at a more general/abstract level, with deeper treatment where appropriate; 4–5 minutes. | confirmed-official | [F-S] |
| Performance is assessed throughout against Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation. | confirmed-official | [F-S], [S-CRITERIA] |
| Four criteria are equally weighted. There are not three separately weighted part scores or one mark per correct opinion. | confirmed-official | [SCORE], [F-S] |
| No official public word count per answer, mandatory idiom count or invariant question total was found. | unverified for any such quota | [F-S], [S-BANDS] |

A text-only interaction can practise organization and language but cannot supply full evidence for pronunciation; a faithful Speaking simulation needs candidate audio and timed turn management. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, implementing the pronunciation construct [S-CRITERIA].

## 2. Every part/task type, prompts and counts

Speaking's task types are the **three interaction patterns**, not TFNG/MCQ-style item blocks. Person/place/object/event/activity/experience are topic families within Part 2, not six exclusive official question codes. **Confidence: confirmed-official for part structure** [F-S]; exhaustive topic taxonomy **unverified**.

| Part | Prompt/input contract | Counts and timings | Authoring rules and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part 1 interview | Examiner topic transition, then brief questions about familiar personal experiences, habits, preferences and reasons. | Official sample: 4 hometown + 4 accommodation questions; not proof that a full test has exactly 8. Default design: three topic frames with roughly 3–4 available questions each; timing controls delivery. | Format confirmed-official [F-S]; sample count confirmed-by-papers [P-S] pp.3–4; three-frame practice supported historically [SCRIPT] §4.1.1, current fixed count unverified. |
| Part 2 long turn | A cue card: main topic, supporting prompts under `You should say:`, an explanation demand, and preparation/speaking instructions. | One card; 60-second preparation; 1–2-minute candidate turn; normally 1–2 rounding-off questions if used. | Format confirmed-official [F-S]; printed task/transcript [P-S] p.5; four visible bullets is not established as an invariant. |
| Part 3 discussion | A transition connecting the long-turn subject to societal/general issues; open questions and contingent follow-ups. | Default design: two linked subthemes, 4–6 principal questions available plus probes. Actual delivered count depends on time/answer length. | Abstract linked discussion confirmed-official [F-S]; sample has two subthemes and 3 main frame questions, with extra transcript probes [P-S] pp.6–7; targets unverified/design recommendation. |

### Part 1 topic frames

Suitable frames include home/accommodation, hometown, work/studies, interests, routines, transport or ordinary technology use. Start with something answerable from the candidate's life rather than a specialist debate. Questions can elicit description, frequency, past change, future preference and justification. **Confidence: confirmed-official for familiar-topic construct**, [F-S]; topic-function menu is **unverified; design recommendation**, illustrated by [P-S] pp.3–4.

A common frame opening uses `Let's talk about ...`; transitions can use `Let's move on to talk about ...`. These are functional script patterns observed in the official specimen, not a claim to have recovered every live frame. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers**, [P-S] p.3.

For a topic-themed app, keep personal entry points broad. “Do you use technology for study?” is more accessible than “Which transformer architectures do you prefer?” Work/study branches should support employed people, students and candidates currently doing neither. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, informed by [F-S], accessibility issues in [SCRIPT].

### Part 2 cue-card layout and explanation line

The official format requires supporting points and explanation of one aspect. The familiar authoring layout is a **Describe ...** stem, three supporting prompts and a final **and explain ...** demand. Use this as a default; do not make the number of typographic bullet glyphs a scoring condition. **Confidence: confirmed-official for explanation requirement** [F-S]; four-line default **unverified; design recommendation**.

The official sample PDF is itself a useful warning: on printed p.5, the displayed card has three visible support lines; text extraction finds an explanation line entangled with the timing footer, but that line is not clearly rendered in the inspected image. Do not reproduce this defect or treat it as evidence that the explanation requirement has disappeared. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers**, visual/text inspection [P-S] p.5; requirement [F-S].

An original practice card following the supported structure:

```text
Describe a piece of technology that has helped you learn something.

You should say:
    what it is
    when you started using it
    how you use it
and explain why it has been useful to you.
```

This is an **original design example**, not an official paper quote. The timing footer/intro should specify one minute to prepare, one to two minutes to speak, and optional notes. Present the complete card before starting preparation. Keep rounding-off questions in examiner materials rather than accidentally merging them into the candidate card. **Confidence: confirmed-official for timing/notes** [F-S]; separation observed [P-S] p.5 and required here as a **design recommendation**.

Other accessible card subjects can involve a memorable place, helpful person, valued object, event or activity. The prompts should all describe **one coherent subject** and allow enough material for an extended turn; they should not demand four disconnected mini-essays. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, applying [F-S], [S-CRITERIA].

### Part 3 abstraction progression

| Progression step | Example linked to the original learning-technology card | Confidence / basis |
|---|---|---|
| Individual case → social practice | How has technology changed the way people learn? | unverified; design example based on general/abstract discussion [F-S] |
| Compare groups or times | Do younger and older learners use it differently? | unverified; design example; historical-change frame observed [P-S] p.6 |
| Explain/evaluate causes or consequences | What might schools gain or lose by relying more on digital tools? | unverified; design example based on analytical construct [F-S] |
| Speculate or consider policy | How might learning change in the next ten years? | unverified; design example; future-status probe observed [P-S] p.6 |

Part 3 need not climb a rigid Bloom-taxonomy ladder, become monotonically harder every question, or demand expert factual answers. It needs opportunities to justify, compare, analyze and speculate. Follow-up requests should depend on what the candidate says while staying within the theme and time. **Confidence: confirmed-official for functions**, [F-S]; progression/contingency policy **unverified; design recommendation**, illustrated in [P-S] pp.6–7.

## 3. Examiner script rules: what is and is not established

| Rule or claim | Confidence | Source / scope |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce examiner, establish identity, give topic transitions and administer the timed long turn. | confirmed-official; sample implementation confirmed-by-papers | [F-S], [P-S] |
| Stop the long turn when the allotted time ends; a stop alone is not evidence of low performance. | confirmed-official | [F-S]; specimen [P-S] p.5 |
| Preserve Part 1 standardized question frames; Part 3 permits more flexible follow-up interaction. | confirmed-by-papers for sample contrast; examiner-claim/historical guideline evidence | [P-S], [SCRIPT] §§4.4.1–4.4.3 |
| Historically, a Part 1 question could be skipped if already answered; first-frame work/study tense could be adapted. | examiner-claim category, with partner guideline check reported | [SCRIPT] §4.1.1, PDF pp.13–15; not a claim that all questions must always be read literally |
| Historically, Part 1 repetition was restricted and rephrasing was not permitted; Part 2 repetition/glossing permissions differed; Part 3 proactive rephrasing was allowed. | examiner-claim category / dated guideline evidence | [SCRIPT] §4.4.4, PDF p.33 cites Instructions to Examiners (2011); exact current permissions unverified |
| Examiner feedback must not become teaching/coaching during the timed test; keep explanations and suggested answers until practice review. | unverified; design recommendation | Preserves interview sample and assessment purpose [F-S], [S-CRITERIA] |
| A simulated examiner should use neutral acknowledgments and targeted clarification/probes rather than supplying a position for the candidate to repeat. | unverified; design recommendation | Official specimen interaction [P-S]; validity concerns [SCRIPT] |
| The examiner must never interrupt, may always simplify every question, or must ask exactly twelve Part 1 questions. | unverified/overbroad | Timing [F-S] and dated permissions/skip discussion [SCRIPT] do not establish these absolutes |

For an app, implement separate **timed test** and **coaching** modes. In test mode, default to repeating the original Part 1 prompt and using constrained Part 3 probes; disclose that this is a simulation policy rather than the exact confidential current script. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, informed by [F-S], [SCRIPT].

## 4. Public descriptors and answer/scoring conventions

### Version caveat

Use the current public [Speaking descriptors][S-BANDS] and [key assessment criteria][S-CRITERIA]. Unlike the Writing PDF, the inspected Speaking PDF does **not** display an “Updated May 2023” label. The brief's May 2023 date is verified for **Writing**; a common revision date for both skills remains **unverified**. Preserve this distinction when building assessment prompts. [W-BANDS], [S-BANDS].

### Criterion definitions

| Criterion | What to assess | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency and Coherence | Continuity, effort, hesitation/repetition, connected development and logical organization; speed alone is insufficient. | confirmed-official [S-CRITERIA], [S-BANDS] |
| Lexical Resource | Range, precision, appropriacy and paraphrase across the topics actually discussed; forced idioms do not supply automatic credit. | confirmed-official [S-CRITERIA], [S-BANDS] |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | Flexible structures and control in spoken production; sentence counts alone are not the rubric. | confirmed-official [S-CRITERIA], [S-BANDS] |
| Pronunciation | Intelligibility and control of phonological features such as stress, rhythm, intonation and connected speech. Native accent imitation is not required. | confirmed-official [S-CRITERIA], [S-BANDS] |

### Compact scale guide

This paraphrase helps orient generation/review; the full four-column descriptor table is the rating authority.

| Band | Broad performance distinction |
|---|---|
| 9 | Effortless, coherent development; highly precise flexible language and pronunciation. |
| 8 | Fluent coherent speech; broad flexible resources and easily understood pronunciation; occasional lapses. |
| 7 | Sustained turns and flexible language; some access problems/errors without major loss of coherence. |
| 6 | Willing sustained speech; sufficient language, variable control, occasional coherence loss. |
| 5 | Repetition/hesitation limit flow; restricted flexibility and uneven complex language. |
| 4 | Basic meaning conveyed with noticeable breakdowns and restricted language. |
| 3 | Short limited responses; frequent difficulty conveying intended meaning. |
| 2 | Isolated words or memorized fragments with very limited communication. |
| 1 | Minimal isolated production; essentially no usable connected speech. |
| 0 | No assessable speaking attempt under source conditions. |

**Confidence: confirmed-official**, [S-BANDS]. Pronunciation has its own distinctions, including bands described through features of neighboring bands; the broad row summaries must not flatten those separate criterion judgments.

### Scoring package

Speaking has **no raw answer-key conversion**, no objectively correct personal opinion and no Task Achievement criterion. A practice package should contain frame/card/discussion prompts, timing/script notes and a criterion-based review guide. Any illustrative answer is one possible response, not a script candidates must reproduce. **Confidence: confirmed-official for criteria** [F-S], [S-CRITERIA]; package policy **unverified; design recommendation**.

Calculate a practice estimate from the four criterion ratings with equal weight; do not invent per-part weights, automatic deductions for every pause, or guaranteed band caps for missing one cue-card point. Assess organization/relevance through the actual descriptors. **Confidence: confirmed-official for weights**, [SCORE]; no fabricated caps is a **design recommendation** based on [S-BANDS].

Spelling/hyphenation are not candidate answer-string gates in an oral interview. Spoken plurals/verb forms can affect grammar; a pronounced number/date must be intelligible, but there is no one-mark slot key. A transcript-only evaluator must mark pronunciation as unavailable rather than claim a full verified Speaking band. **Confidence: confirmed-official for constructs**, [S-CRITERIA]; evaluator disclosure **unverified; design recommendation**.

## 5. Claims to reject or qualify

| Claim | Verdict | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|
| Every cue card must show exactly four bullet glyphs. | Use supporting prompts plus explanation; typographic count is not a verified universal requirement. | confirmed-official construct [F-S]; layout variation/defect [P-S] p.5 |
| Part 2 alone determines the Speaking band. | Incorrect; performance is assessed throughout. | confirmed-official [F-S] |
| Each cue-card point earns one mark. | Incorrect analytic-scoring model. | confirmed-official [S-CRITERIA], [SCORE] |
| Candidates need a British accent or a fixed number of idioms. | Not the public scoring requirements; intelligibility and appropriate language matter. | confirmed-official [S-BANDS] |
| Part 3 is a knowledge quiz about the chosen specialist topic. | Wrong construct; it elicits explanation, analysis, discussion and speculation. | confirmed-official [F-S] |
| All examiner turns are improvised / every turn is rigidly identical. | Both overgeneralize; part-dependent structure and dated permissions differ. | confirmed-by-papers [P-S]; historical evidence [SCRIPT] |
| Speaking descriptors were necessarily revised in May 2023 alongside Writing. | Date not verified in the inspected Speaking source. | unverified; [S-BANDS], [W-BANDS] |
