# IELTS Academic Writing: construction rules

Checked 11 October 2026. [Sources and confidence definitions](sources.md). This report concerns Academic visual description, not General Training letters. Official sample booklet page numbers refer to [P-W].

## 1. Format and scoring contract

| Rule | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Two tasks in 60 minutes; both must be answered. Task 1 requires at least 150 words; Task 2 at least 250. | confirmed-official | [F-W] |
| Printed suggested allocations are about 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 for Task 2; these are not separately locked test timers. | confirmed-official for guidance; unverified for a separate lock | [F-W], [P-W] pp.3–7 |
| Task 1 describes supplied visual information; Task 2 responds to a point of view, argument or problem. | confirmed-official | [F-W] |
| Connected prose in academic or semi-formal/neutral style; neither task is a bullet-list answer. | confirmed-official | [F-W] |
| Task 2 contributes twice Task 1's weight. Four analytic criteria are equally weighted within each task. | confirmed-official | [F-W], [SCORE] |
| Writing minima are not maxima. No universal 170–190/270–290 word requirement or fixed paragraph count is published in the format. | confirmed-official for minima; unverified for proposed exact ranges | [F-W] |
| A specialist theme must be answerable through supplied Task 1 information or general experience/reasoning in Task 2. | unverified; design recommendation | Derived from [F-W], [W-CRITERIA], topic balance [DEV] |

## 2. Task 1: every visual family

### Prompt contract

The printed Task 1 wrapper contains the task label, approximately 20-minute guidance, a stimulus-description sentence, a summarization directive and the 150-word minimum. A familiar directive is `Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.` **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers**, [P-W] pp.3–5. Do not add a question requiring opinions, causes absent from the visual, or background research. **Confidence: confirmed-official for information-transfer construct**, [W-CRITERIA].

Stimulus introductions identify what is shown, the population/place and relevant date/period; change singular/plural nouns to match the actual display. Do not mix “percentage”, “number” and “thousands” in prompt/axis/legend. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers for specimen construction**, [P-W] pp.3–5; consistency gate is **unverified; design recommendation**.

| Visual family | What the stimulus must make describable | Proposed density default | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line graph | Changes, peaks/troughs, crossings, start/end relationships; meaningful time axis and units. | 2–4 series; 4–8 major plotted times, or a clearly readable continuous daily timeline. | confirmed-official partner family [W-VISUAL]; density unverified/design recommendation |
| Bar chart | Comparisons across categories, groups or periods; bars may be horizontal, vertical or grouped. | 4–8 categories or a grouped display with about 12–24 values. | family confirmed-official [W-VISUAL]; sample 12 bars confirmed-by-papers [P-W] p.3; default unverified |
| Pie chart | Part-to-whole distribution; comparisons across groups/times when multiple pies are supplied. | 4–6 segments per pie; 1–3 pies, with total and rounding convention clear. | family confirmed-official partner guidance [W-VISUAL]; numeric target unverified/design recommendation |
| Table | Numeric comparisons across labelled rows/columns; clear unit and period headings. | 4–6 rows × 2–4 data columns; avoid a wall of unrelated figures. | family confirmed-official [F-W], [W-VISUAL]; density unverified/design recommendation |
| Process: manufactured | Inputs, transformations, alternative branches, main sequence and final output; arrows establish flow. | Approximately 5–9 major stages; include branches only when useful. | family confirmed-official [F-W]; branching brick process confirmed-by-papers [P-W] p.5; density unverified |
| Process: natural/cyclic | Stages, conditions and return path, with start/end or cyclic status clear. | Approximately 5–9 visible stages; variable by process. | family confirmed-official partner guidance [W-VISUAL]; density unverified/design recommendation |
| Map/site plan | Locations, additions/removals/replacements and unchanged features, or comparison of alternative layouts. | Often 2 layouts/time states; 6–10 salient features is a usable target. | family confirmed-official partner guidance [W-VISUAL], [W-TYPES]; exact layout/features quota unverified |
| Mixed visuals | Two compatible views of related information, with clear connections and units. | 2 visuals; reduce density in each so both can be covered in the allotted time. | family confirmed-official partner guidance [W-VISUAL]; target unverified/design recommendation |
| Object/device/event diagram | Structural relationships or how something works, when not neatly classified as a statistical chart/map/process. | Enough labelled components to explain the depicted function; no fixed count. | confirmed-official [F-W]; density policy unverified/design recommendation |

These are stimulus families, not objectively keyed item blocks: a complete Writing test includes **one Task 1**, which can itself contain multiple visuals. No word limit is printed for a chart label as if it were a Reading gap. **Confidence: confirmed-official**, [F-W].

### Directly inspected density examples

- Task 1A, [P-W] p.3: grouped bars for men/women × full-/part-time × three academic-year periods = **12 displayed bars**, with an axis in thousands. **confirmed-by-papers**.
- Task 1B, [P-W] p.4: two audience series across a daily timeline, illustrating that “four dates” is not a universal rule. **confirmed-by-papers** for stimulus description; a detailed point count is not asserted.
- Task 1C, [P-W] p.5: brick manufacture with two shaping routes, drying, kiln temperature phases, cooling, packaging and delivery. Counting “stages” depends on grouping; the sample commentary's seven-step grouping is not a universal process specification. **confirmed-by-papers**.

Only 1A and 1C were visually audited for geometry/data density. The other families are supported by official/partner format descriptions; no distribution across all Cambridge books is invented.

### Key features and overview

| Rule | Confidence | Evidence / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The task must permit selection of important features rather than demand a recital of every number. | confirmed-official | [F-W], [W-CRITERIA] |
| An overview identifies the major pattern: overall trends/differences for data, major transformations for maps, or broad sequence/input/output for processes. It is not merely a restatement of the chart title. | confirmed-official for overview construct; application examples are design recommendations | [F-W], [W-BANDS], [W-VISUAL] |
| Band 7 Task Achievement explicitly includes a clear overview, appropriate grouping and recognition of principal trends/differences. Band 6 attempts a relevant overview. | confirmed-official | [W-BANDS], Task 1 pp.1–2 within the rubric |
| An overview is not prescribed to be exactly two sentences, placed only at the end or free of all numbers. | unverified as rigid prescription | [F-W], [W-BANDS]; [W-VISUAL] allows different placements |
| Figures support key features but need not all be mentioned. Include sufficient accurate comparisons to distinguish stronger responses. | confirmed-official | [F-W], [W-BANDS]; specimen comments [P-W] pp.9–12 |
| Process/map overviews must fit their visual semantics; “everything increased” cannot substitute for identifying a sequence or redevelopment. | unverified; design recommendation | Application of [W-BANDS], [W-VISUAL] |
| Synthetic data may be used for practice if internally coherent and identified as such; never present invented figures as an actual cited study. | unverified; design recommendation | Original-stimulus policy informed by [W-CRITERIA], [DEV] |

## 3. Task 2: prompt families and wording

### Printed wrapper

The Task 2 paper contains its task label, approximately 40-minute guidance, topic introduction, the premise and actual question(s), an instruction to justify the response with relevant examples from knowledge/experience, and the 250-word minimum. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers**, [P-W] pp.6–7. Examples are invited; real research citations and named studies are not required. **Confidence: confirmed-official**, [F-W], [W-CRITERIA].

A premise should be concise, comprehensible and debatable without privileged knowledge. The question must tell the candidate what stance/comparison/explanation is required. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, informed by [F-W], [W-PROMPTS].

| Teaching family | Familiar prompt wording pattern | What must be answerable | Confidence / sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion / agree-disagree | `To what extent do you agree or disagree?` An example adds “with this opinion”. | A definite proposition; degrees of agreement may be defended. Do not force agreement and disagreement paragraphs as a scoring rule. | patterns confirmed-official partner guidance [W-PROMPTS], confirmed-by-papers [P-W] p.6; paragraph restriction unverified |
| Discussion + opinion | `Discuss both views and give your own opinion.` | Two meaningfully distinct views and a candidate position. Merely describing two views omits an explicit requested opinion. | confirmed-official partner prompt example [W-PROMPTS] |
| Problem–solution / causes–solutions | Partner examples: `What are the problems associated with this?`; `What can be done to solve this?` Causes/reasons are a different possible demand. | Assess the actual problems, causes, effects or interventions requested; do not infer causes from a problems question. | confirmed-official partner prompt fragments [W-PROBLEMS]; not a quote from inspected P-W |
| Advantages–disadvantages | `What are the advantages and disadvantages of ...?` | Both benefits and drawbacks; no mandatory “which outweighs” decision unless asked. | confirmed-official partner prompt example [W-PROMPTS] |
| Outweigh / comparative evaluation | `Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?` Also “Do you think ...” and reversed disadvantages/advantages variants. | Comparative judgment supported by both sides; differs from simple enumeration. | confirmed-official partner prompt pattern [W-EVALUATE], [W-OUTWEIGH]; reversed specimen confirmed-by-papers [P-W] p.7 |
| Positive/negative development | `Is this a positive or negative development?` | Evaluate the specified trend, allowing a qualified position. | confirmed-official partner prompt example [W-PROMPTS], [W-EVALUATE]; not claimed as a separate inspected operational paper |
| Two-part / direct questions | A pair such as “Why has this happened?” and “What effects does it have?” | Two distinct answer obligations; questions can mix explanation, effects, solutions or evaluation. | confirmed-official partner guidance [W-PROMPTS] |
| Hybrid multi-part + opinion | An explanatory question followed by whether problems outweigh benefits, or another stance request. | Every actual question; select response obligations from the wording, not a rigid category label. | confirmed-official partner example [W-PROMPTS] |

**Exactly five exclusive families is not an official construction law.** IDP teaches five common categories, while IELTS's prompt guidance includes hybrids and emphasizes answering the actual wording. The generator should represent answer obligations (discuss view A, discuss view B, state opinion, explain cause, assess effect, propose solution, compare benefits/costs), allowing combinations. **Confidence: confirmed-official partner guidance** [W-FAMILIES], [W-PROMPTS]; obligation representation is **unverified; design recommendation**.

Do not use narrow topic requests to make a medical, financial or technical expertise exam. An AI-lab Task 2 could ask about publicly funded research priorities or student access to technology, with the necessary issue stated in ordinary language. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, based on [F-W], [DEV].

## 4. Public band descriptors and evaluation

### Version and weighting

The authoritative [Writing band-descriptor PDF][W-BANDS] is explicitly **Updated May 2023**. Its Task 1 and Task 2 tables are separate. [W-REVISION] explains the review history. Older model-answer comments can illustrate performance but must not override the revised descriptors. **Confidence: confirmed-official**.

Task 1 criteria: Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Task 2 substitutes Task Response for Task Achievement. Each has equal criterion weight; a conceptual practice calculation is task means followed by `(Task 1 + 2 × Task 2) / 3`. Do not invent official internal rounding rules from the separate rule for rounding the four-skill overall score. **Confidence: confirmed-official for criterion/task weights** [SCORE], [F-W]; exact automated rating/rounding policy **unverified; design recommendation**.

### Descriptor guide, not a substitute rubric

This compact guide paraphrases the public scale; consult **all four columns** of the source when rating. It does not supply new numeric feature thresholds.

| Band | Task fulfillment emphasis | Language/organization emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Requirements fulfilled in depth with negligible content lapses. | Effortless organization, precise flexible vocabulary and grammar, very rare errors. |
| 8 | Sufficient relevant coverage and well-developed/selectively illustrated content. | Easy progression, broad flexible language; occasional lapses. |
| 7 | Task 1 clear overview; Task 2 developed position and supported main ideas. | Logical progression, flexible cohesion and complex language; some imprecision. |
| 6 | Relevant coverage but uneven development; Task 1 overview attempted. | Generally clear meaning; limited flexibility and mechanical cohesion/errors. |
| 5 | Incomplete coverage or development; key features/position inadequately handled. | Limited language; organization and errors can impede the reader. |
| 4 | Minimal/inaccurate handling of major requirements. | Weak progression and restricted, error-prone language. |
| 3 | Prompt largely misunderstood or inadequately addressed. | Very limited communication and severe organization/language weaknesses. |
| 2 | Barely related content. | Mostly inadequate lexical/grammatical resources. |
| 1 | Extremely little assessable relevant language. | Minimal isolated language. |
| 0 | No assessable attempt under the descriptor's stated conditions. | Follow source conditions; do not infer solely from a topic mismatch. |

**Confidence: confirmed-official**, paraphrase of [W-BANDS]. Full descriptors, including lower-band length/assessment conditions and negative features that limit criterion ratings, are the authority; the summary must not be used as a deterministic essay scorer.

### Answer package and marking conventions

Writing has **no single correct answer string** or raw-score conversion. The generator should provide a task-specific feature/obligation checklist, one illustrative response, and evidence-based notes against each criterion. A model response is not the only valid position or organization. **Confidence: confirmed-official for analytic assessment** [F-W], [W-CRITERIA]; package structure **unverified; design recommendation**.

For Task 1, list key trends/comparisons/stages and tolerate reasonable grouping or graph-reading approximation; verify the reference response against every supplied value. For Task 2, distinguish missing requested obligations from a defensible opinion the assessor disagrees with. These are **unverified; design recommendations**, grounded in [W-BANDS], [W-CRITERIA].

Spelling, word formation and grammatical number affect the analytic language criteria; they are not one-mark deductions per error. Plagiarism, irrelevant material and inappropriate format are addressed in official guidance. Underlength can limit evidence of proficiency, but no universal fixed “minus one band” algorithm was verified. **Confidence: confirmed-official guidance** [F-W], [W-BANDS]; fixed penalty claim **unverified**.

## 5. Claims to reject or qualify

| Claim | Verdict | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|
| Every chart has six categories or three dates. | Unsupported; numeric targets above are authoring choices. | unverified quota; specific specimen [P-W] p.3 |
| A Task 1 overview must be exactly two sentences and contain no figures. | Not a verified descriptor requirement. | unverified [W-BANDS], [W-VISUAL] |
| Every process has exactly seven steps. | A sample's grouping, not a universal rule. | confirmed-by-papers grouping [P-W] p.17; quota unverified |
| Every Task 2 essay has four paragraphs and two examples. | Teaching scaffold, not official task or scoring contract. | unverified [F-W], [W-BANDS] |
| Every advantages/disadvantages question requires an opinion. | The actual question decides; enumeration and outweigh prompts differ. | confirmed-official partner guidance [W-PROMPTS] |
| No overview caps the entire Writing result at band 5/6. | Confuses a criterion weakness with task/section weighting; sample comments also illustrate different overall outcomes. | confirmed-official weighting [SCORE]; samples [P-W] pp.13–17 |
| Task 1 may invent reasons explaining graph changes. | Outside the intended supplied-information task unless represented in the stimulus. | confirmed-official [W-CRITERIA] |
| A fluent LLM model essay proves the prompt is IELTS-equivalent. | It tests neither learner accessibility nor calibrated difficulty. | confirmed-official development requirements [DEV] |
