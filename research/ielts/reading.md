# IELTS Academic Reading: construction rules

Checked 11 October 2026. Confidence labels and corpus limitations: [sources.md](sources.md). Numbered pages below are the printed pages of [P-R], not browser page indexes. All proposed numeric defaults are **design recommendations**, not undisclosed IELTS specifications.

## 1. Official format and text selection

| Rule | Confidence | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Three sections/texts, 40 numbered questions, 60 minutes including answer transfer; one mark per correct response. | confirmed-official | [F-R], [SCORE] |
| Total passage text is 2,150–2,750 words. There is no published equal-length requirement or exact per-passage quota. | confirmed-official for total; unverified for any fixed per-passage quota | [F-R] |
| Source genres include books, journals, magazines, newspapers and online material, aimed at non-specialists entering higher education. At least one text contains detailed logical argument. | confirmed-official | [F-R] |
| Technical vocabulary needs a simple definition when used as specified by the format guidance; images/graphs may accompany texts. | confirmed-official | [F-R]; dung-beetle glossary, [P-R] p.6 |
| Later passages should generally place greater comprehension demands on readers; this does not imply every item in Passage 3 is harder than every item in Passage 1. | confirmed-official as older partner teaching guidance; not a universal item-level law | [BC-DIFFICULTY], [DEV] |
| A useful authoring allocation is roughly 700–950 words per passage, with the three-passage total checked separately. Equal lengths are unnecessary. | unverified; design recommendation | Inference from total in [F-R], not a measured Cambridge distribution |
| Use a 13/13/14 item allocation as a convenient default; any proposed allocation must total 40. | unverified; design recommendation | [F-R] gives total, not fixed per-passage allocation |
| Topic-themed passages must still be independently answerable and vary genre/angle; an AI-labs theme could cover history, experimental methodology and an argument about research incentives. | unverified; design recommendation | Adaptation of non-specialist scope [F-R] and topic/genre balance [DEV] |

Avoid fabricating a magazine attribution for an original passage. Record genuine source provenance or identify original practice material. Specialist knowledge must not be needed to answer the items. These are generator quality requirements derived from the intended construct, not claims that IELTS publishes a particular source-credit template. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation**, informed by [F-R], [IW].

## 2. Task instructions and complete question-type inventory

IELTS lists **eleven umbrella types**. Splitting MCQ selection modes and the four completion layouts produces more surface types; “14 question types” is a teaching count, not a competing test specification. **Confidence: confirmed-official** [F-R].

Instruction fragments below preserve the functional printed wording. `{a}`, `{b}`, `{letters}`, `{roman}` and `{limit}` are generator substitutions, not text printed literally. Add the appropriate numbered answer-sheet routing line in paper practice, or adapt routing to the screen. Never impose a word limit on a letter-selection task. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers** [P-R] task pages; screen variant [HUB].

| Type | Printed directive/pattern and response mode | Observed block; suggested target | Construction rule, confidence and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| MCQ: one answer | `Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.` Letter per number. | 4 items, p.28; target 3–5. | One correct option among four; alternatives must compete on meaning, not just keyword presence. Chronology applies within the block. Format/order confirmed-official [F-R]; sample/count confirmed-by-papers [P-R]; distractor uniqueness is a design recommendation. |
| MCQ: multiple answers | `Choose TWO letters, A-G.` Substitute actual selection count and range; one numbered slot per selected answer in the inspected paper. | Two separate 2-slot tasks with seven/six options, p.25; target 2–3 selections per task. | Exactly the requested number supported; key the selection as an unordered set when the source specifies either order. Two choices do not make a single 1-mark item by default. confirmed-by-papers [P-R] pp.25–26; allowed multi-selection confirmed-official [F-R]. |
| Identifying information: TFNG | `Do the following statements agree with the information given in Reading Passage {n}?` Labels TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN. | 3 items, p.13; target 4–7. | TRUE = supported; FALSE = contradicted; NOT GIVEN = neither established nor contradicted. Judge passage information, not outside facts. confirmed-official [F-R]; count confirmed-by-papers [P-R]. |
| Identifying views/claims: YNNG | `Do the following statements reflect the claims of the writer in Sample Passage 8?` Official example; use a matching passage reference. Labels YES/NO/NOT GIVEN. | 4 items, Cambridge activity p.10; target 4–7. | Judge the attributed writer's position, not whether a reported belief is scientifically true. Other official wording uses “agree with the views/claims”. confirmed-official [F-R]; printed example/count confirmed-by-papers [YNNG]. |
| Matching information | `Which paragraph contains the following information?` A paragraph/section letter per number. | No dedicated block in inspected P-R; no claimed empirical range. Target 4–6. | Details/examples/reasons, rather than a paragraph's main idea. Not every paragraph must be used; repetition is allowed only under the appropriate reuse instruction. confirmed-official [F-R]; older example [CONSTRUCT] PDF p.21; target is design recommendation. |
| Matching headings | `Choose the correct heading for each section from the list of headings below.` Roman numeral response. | Four scored sections + one example, eight headings, p.15; target 5–7 scored paragraphs. | More headings than sections/paragraphs; no heading used twice. Examples are optional, already solved and not scored. Test main idea, not incidental detail. confirmed-official [F-R]; specific arrangement confirmed-by-papers [P-R] pp.15–18. |
| Matching features | Prompt establishes the relation; select a letter with a line such as `Write the correct letter A-E in boxes 7-10 on your answer sheet.` Add `NB You may use any letter more than once.` when applicable. Adapt ranges/numbers. | 4 items, five group options, p.4; target 4–6. | Match person/group/date/theory to the correct relationship. Distractors can be unused and options reusable under explicit instructions; not necessarily in passage order. confirmed-official [F-R]; instruction/construction observed [P-R] pp.3–5. |
| Matching sentence endings | `Complete each sentence with the correct ending, A-F, below.` Letter response. | 3 beginnings, six endings, p.21; target 3–5. | More endings than beginnings; beginnings follow source order. Completed sentence must be both grammatical and supported; grammatical fit alone should not solve the entire task. Surplus/order confirmed-official [F-R]; sample confirmed-by-papers [P-R]; latter quality gate is design recommendation. |
| Sentence completion | `Complete the sentences below.` Follow with extraction instruction and limit. | 5 items, ONE WORD ONLY, p.36; target 3–6. | Extract words from text; stems may paraphrase. Questions follow text order. Missing words must fit the sentence without inflection changes. confirmed-official [F-R]; exact extraction observed [P-R] pp.34–37. |
| Summary completion: text extraction | `Complete the summary below.` Extraction instruction, not an option-letter response. | 5 gaps, two-word maximum, p.42; target 4–6. | Usually a portion of the passage; gap sequence need not equal text sequence. Preserve lexical answer while paraphrasing the surrounding summary. confirmed-official [F-R]; sample [P-R] pp.41–43. |
| Summary completion: word box | `Complete the summary using the list of words, A-G, below.` Letter response in this sample. | 4 gaps, seven choices, p.39; target 4–6. | Options can represent paraphrases; do not require the chosen word to occur verbatim in the passage. Decide response mode explicitly. Surplus observed, not a published fixed ratio. confirmed-official for two variants [F-R]; confirmed-by-papers [P-R] pp.38–40. |
| Note completion | `Complete the notes below.` Extraction instruction and limit. | 6 gaps, ONE WORD ONLY, p.32; target 4–7. | Notes should have coherent hierarchy and summarize relevant detail. No universal source-order guarantee for this family. confirmed-official [F-R]; sample [P-R] pp.30–33. |
| Table completion | `Complete the table below.` Extraction instruction and limit. | 5 gaps, three-word maximum, p.7; target 4–6. | Rows/columns define meaningful shared categories; units/headings constrain gaps. Traversal order is not guaranteed to match passage order. confirmed-official [F-R]; sample [P-R] pp.6–8. |
| Flow-chart completion | `Complete the flow-chart below.` Extraction instruction and limit, or an explicitly specified list-selection mode. | 3 gaps, two-word maximum, p.10; target 3–6. | Arrows encode process/causal sequence; branches must match the described process. A flow chart's logical sequence need not be the passage's exposition sequence. confirmed-official [F-R]; branch layout [P-R] pp.9–11. |
| Diagram label completion | `Label the diagram below.` Extraction instruction and limit. | 5 gaps, three-word maximum, p.45; target 3–6. | Labels map a textual description to specific visible parts; usually a localized passage region, but labels need not follow source order. confirmed-official [F-R]; diagram/key [P-R] pp.44–46. |
| Short-answer questions | Answer factual questions using words/numbers from the passage; an appropriate template is `Answer the questions below.` + extraction limit. | No dedicated block in P-R; target 3–5, with no measured frequency claim. | Source extraction; chronological questions; digits or number words allowed as specified by the format guidance. confirmed-official [F-R]; directive here is a reconstructed template, not an inspected Reading-paper quote. |

All **suggested targets** above are **unverified design recommendations**; the official count per block is “variable”. Do not treat sample extracts as complete papers, concatenate their question numbers, or include every type in every 40-item test. [F-R], [P-R], [DEV].

### Response contract and word-limit wording

Use an extraction line with the actual permitted maximum, such as `Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.` A one-word sample uses `Choose ONE WORD ONLY from the passage for each answer.` The official number-inclusive pattern is `NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER`. “AND/OR” permits words, a number, or both; “AND” and “OR” must not be silently interchanged. **Confidence: confirmed-official** [F-R]; shorter extraction limits **confirmed-by-papers** [P-R] pp.7, 10, 32, 36, 42, 45.

The paper routing line identifies the actual numbered boxes and answer sheet. Label legends must define all three alternatives in TFNG/YNNG; print the legend appropriate to **information** or **writer's position**. Do not silently accept TRUE where YES was requested. **Confidence: confirmed-by-papers** [P-R] p.13, [YNNG] p.10; strict response-mode enforcement is a **design recommendation**.

## 3. Structural and unwritten rules

### Passage-order matrix

Interpret a stated ordering rule **within its task block**; do not infer one global sequence across different blocks. Blocks may cover overlapping passage regions. **Confidence: unverified; design recommendation** for cross-block assembly; Marie Curie is independently used for TFNG and notes [P-R] pp.12–14, 30–33, but those separate extracts do not demonstrate the ordering of a complete paper.

| Type | Source-order requirement | Confidence and source |
|---|---|---|
| Single-answer MCQ; sentence endings; sentence completion; short answers | Officially described as source ordered. Global-title MCQ or a multi-selection set needs separate handling because it may refer to a whole region rather than one successive sentence. | confirmed-official [F-R]; multi-selection either-order key [P-R] p.26 |
| TFNG/YNNG | Cambridge's teaching worksheet explicitly describes text order; use ordered anchor regions. NOT GIVEN has a relevant comparison/topic region, not a literal supporting answer span. Do not attribute an explicit order promise to the current IELTS format page for these two types. | confirmed-official dated teaching guidance [YNNG] p.6; confirmed-by-papers TFNG block [P-R] pp.12–14; partner “usually” guidance [R-TYPES]; NG anchoring is a design recommendation |
| Matching headings | Questions generally list paragraphs/sections in their document order; the heading list is shuffled. This is not chronological answer extraction. | confirmed-by-papers [P-R] p.15 |
| Matching information; matching features | Scanning/matching relation; impose no source-order guarantee. | confirmed-official task descriptions [F-R]; feature example [P-R] pp.3–5 |
| Summary/note/table/flow chart completion | May depart from passage order; usually concentrated in one portion. | confirmed-official [F-R] |
| Diagram labels | May depart from passage order; usually concentrated in one portion. | confirmed-official [F-R] |

### Item-writing decisions

| Rule | Confidence | Evidence / implementation consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Each extracted answer must have an exact source form fitting the printed gap and limit. Paraphrase the stem, not the required answer. | confirmed-official | [F-R]; “first-time user” in summary key demonstrates a hyphenated source phrase [P-R] p.43. |
| Word-box completion is an exception to the claim that every Reading answer is copied from the text. | confirmed-official | Two distinct modes [F-R], illustrated [P-R] pp.38–40. |
| FALSE/NO requires a contradiction; absence alone belongs to NOT GIVEN. A keyword can be present in all three cases. | confirmed-official | [F-R], [YNNG]. |
| Avoid changing two independent propositions in one binary-verification statement; narrow scope/quantifiers enough to support a unique decision. | unverified; design recommendation | Operationalization of verification construct in [F-R], [CONSTRUCT]. |
| Do not require every option at least once in each TFNG/YNNG key: no public quota was verified. The three-item Curie example contains all three, which proves possibility, not necessity. | unverified as a universal quota; design recommendation not to enforce it | [P-R] pp.13–14, [F-R]; no counterexample is claimed here. |
| Build plausible MCQ competitors around a changed agent, condition, comparison, time or causal direction, and document why each fails. Avoid two synonymous correct options. | unverified; design recommendation | Distractor-writing difficulties studied in [IW]; task examples [P-R] p.28. |
| Matching-heading competitors should share topic vocabulary while differing in the paragraph's central function: proposal, explanation, objection, result. | unverified; design recommendation | Main/support distinction [F-R]; task [P-R] pp.15–18; paragraph-level construct [CONSTRUCT]. |
| No heading used for a solved example may also key a scored paragraph; the example must remain outside the 40 scored responses. | confirmed-official for no heading reuse; design recommendation for explicit counting | [F-R], [P-R] p.15. |
| Text difficulty alone does not determine item difficulty; task, inference, evidence span and distractor competition matter. | confirmed-by-papers for historical research findings; design recommendation for generator review | [IW] §§5–6; [DEV] includes individual-item difficulty balance. |

## 4. Answer key and scoring

### Correctness and acceptable variants

| Convention | Confidence and source |
|---|---|
| Exact spelling and appropriate grammatical number matter. Do not use fuzzy semantic similarity to replace an extraction key. | confirmed-official for correctness guidance [F-R], [SPELL]; exact-key gate is design recommendation |
| Hyphenated words count as one; contracted forms are not tested in the relevant completion types. | confirmed-official [F-R] |
| Numbers can be entered as figures or number words for short answers; don't invent universal date/unit/punctuation normalization beyond the keyed variants. | confirmed-official for figures/words [F-R]; conservative normalization is design recommendation |
| Block capitals are permitted. Case-insensitive comparison is a reasonable practice policy; keep spelling intact. | confirmed-official partner guidance [CAPS]; comparison policy is design recommendation |
| Keys may list slash-separated alternatives and bracketed optional material. Inspect the complete permissible variants before applying the limit. | confirmed-by-papers [P-R] pp.8, 43, 46; alternative expansion is design recommendation |
| Some single gaps have multiple valid phrases; do not hard-code every blank to exactly one string. | confirmed-by-papers: diagram key alternatives [P-R] p.46 |
| Multi-select answers explicitly marked either order are unordered, with one mark per distinct accepted answer slot. Never credit a duplicate choice twice. | confirmed-by-papers [P-R] p.26; duplicate protection is design recommendation |

Store each item's canonical key, accepted alternatives, word-limit contract, source span(s), and rationale. For NOT GIVEN, store what relation is missing and check the **whole passage** for accidental confirmation/contradiction. This storage/review requirement is **unverified; design recommendation**, grounded in [F-R], [P-R].

### Raw score to band: reference estimates

Official IELTS gives approximate anchor marks (Academic Reading: 15→5, 23→6, 30→7, 35→8) and says precise boundaries vary by version. IDP's published range table below uses **16–19 for band 5**, demonstrating why one universal conversion must not be claimed. **Confidence: confirmed-official**, [SCORE], [SCORE-IDP].

| Correct / 40 | Estimated band, IDP reference |
|---|---|
| 39–40 | 9.0 |
| 37–38 | 8.5 |
| 35–36 | 8.0 |
| 33–34 | 7.5 |
| 30–32 | 7.0 |
| 27–29 | 6.5 |
| 23–26 | 6.0 |
| 20–22 | 5.5 |
| 16–19 | 5.0 |
| 13–15 | 4.5 |
| 10–12 | 4.0 |

Lower reference ranges 7–9→3.5 and 5–6→3.0 also appear in [CHANGE-2020]'s Academic Reading table. A current authoritative exhaustive 0–40 conversion was not verified; **0–4 has no asserted mapping here**. Do not confuse this with IELTS band 0's general description of non-attempt. **Confidence: confirmed-official for published reference rows; unverified for universal/exhaustive conversion**. Use the raw score as the primary practice result and label band estimates provisional.

Reading has no public examiner-applied analytic band-descriptor rubric equivalent to Writing/Speaking: answers are scored objectively and converted. General user-level band descriptions do not specify how to author individual items. **Confidence: confirmed-official**, [SCORE].

## 5. Claims to reject or qualify

| Repeated claim | Verdict | Confidence / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Every TFNG/YNNG block must contain each of its three answers. | Unsupported; do not impose a quota or infer a missing answer by distribution. | unverified [F-R], [P-R] |
| All Reading answers occur in passage order. | False for the published format; summaries/diagrams explicitly allow exceptions. | confirmed-official [F-R] |
| Completion always means exact words from the passage. | True for extraction tasks; false as a statement covering word-box mode. | confirmed-official [F-R] |
| Every passage is 900 words, exactly 13 questions and exactly three blocks. | Not an official public rule; total count is 40, so three equal 13-item passages cannot form a complete test. | confirmed-official total [F-R]; quotas unverified |
| The argumentative passage must always be Passage 3. | At least one logical argument is required; its location was not publicly fixed in the inspected format. | confirmed-official requirement / unverified location [F-R] |
| Heading examples are obligatory and all paragraphs must be tested. | False: examples and omitted paragraphs are explicitly possible. | confirmed-official [F-R] |
| A readability score or long scientific terminology proves IELTS difficulty. | Insufficient; review and learner evidence are needed. | confirmed-official process [DEV]; historical findings [IW] |
