# Task 2 prompt families in Cambridge IELTS 12–21

Coverage: 40 Academic Task 2 prompts, four tests per book across Cambridge 12–21. The Cambridge 12–20 local text and Cambridge 21 Ppo text were used for prompt obligations; the existing corpus.md topic summaries were the index. Counts below use one exclusive primary family per prompt. A hybrid combines two distinct answer formats or obligations in one prompt, such as a cause question plus a separate positive/negative judgment.

| Family | Frequency | Wording pattern with slots | Answer obligations | Topic domains seen |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Opinion | 10 | A claim about [topic]. “To what extent do you agree or disagree?” | State and support a definite position; qualify it if useful. | Foreign-language life; choice overload; music and social cohesion; print versus online access; sugar pricing and health; science and public benefit; shorter work weeks; saving; free household water; high-rise housing. |
| Discussion plus opinion | 11 | [Some people hold view A]. Others hold [view B]. “Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.” | Explain both views fairly and state the candidate’s position. | Information sharing; transport investment; school subjects; children’s decisions; accepting or improving hardship; environmental priorities; advertising; skilled-worker migration; broader versus specialized study; competition/cooperation; theatres and cinemas. |
| Problem/cause-solution | 1 | [Problem/context]. “Why is this the case? What can be done about this problem?” | Explain causes and propose relevant actions. Do not substitute a different question such as effects. | Food insecurity despite agricultural advances. |
| Advantages-disadvantages | 1 | [Practice/message]. “What are the advantages and disadvantages?” | Cover both benefits and drawbacks; no weighing decision unless asked. | Telling children effort can achieve anything. |
| Outweigh | 6 | [Situation/policy]. “Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?” | Compare both sides and make a clear comparative judgment. | Age balance between countries; professional risk-taking; driverless vehicles; ageing populations; flying less; required overseas study or work placement. |
| Positive-negative | 3 | [Development/trend]. “Do you think this is a positive or negative development?” | Evaluate the specified development and justify the judgment. | Use of alternative medicine instead of a usual doctor; rural-to-city migration; international food in supermarkets. |
| Two-part direct questions | 3 | [Situation]. “[Why/How] [first demand]? [What/How] [second demand]?” | Answer each direct question explicitly; keep the two parts distinct. | Self-employment causes and disadvantages; researching a home’s history; value of long school holidays and arguments for shorter ones. |
| Hybrids | 5 | [Why/How] [phenomenon]? [Separate agree/disagree or positive/negative judgment]. | Meet every obligation, including both explanation and the additional stance/evaluation. | Home ownership; product novelty; children’s smartphone use; global fashion; formal learning and classroom play. |

Hybrids occurred in 5 of 40 prompts (12.5%): Cambridge 15 Test 1, Cambridge 16 Test 2, Cambridge 17 Test 2, Cambridge 20 Test 4 and Cambridge 21 Test 4. The primary-family counts sum to 40.

## Classification examples

- Cambridge 13 Test 4 is problem/cause-solution because it asks why food insecurity persists and what should be done.
- Cambridge 16 Test 1 and Cambridge 20 Test 2 are two-part direct prompts: both questions need answers, but neither adds a stance/evaluation format.
- Cambridge 21 Test 4 is hybrid: it asks for agreement about formal learning and separately asks how important classroom play is.

Topic names are paraphrased from the prompt and the existing corpus.md summary. Prompt patterns contain only short phrase fragments plus slots; no full prompt is reproduced.
